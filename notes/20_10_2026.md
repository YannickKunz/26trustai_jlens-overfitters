### Preliminary Works

The idea, there is a "workspace" inside the model. When a language model processes a prompt, its internal activations contains a lot of information, most is routine.

**J-lens papers**: In this paper they proposed that only a priviliged set of these representations forms a kind of "workspace" that holds concepts the model use for resoning. The name "workspace" come from neuroscience, where the mind has many specialized modules that share information trough a limited central "workspace".

To read this workspace, the paper built a tool called **J-lens**. Their use the lens to analyse the workspace and show that if you swap things in the internal representation of a model, the model answer flips. That shows that the model really use these hidden steps.

**J-lens++ paper**: This paper claim, there is a problem. The J-lens measures with a tool called a [Jacobian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_matrix_and_determinant). Then it average the measurement over a lot of text to get one general translator from the middle layer to words. It results that some of the measurements going into the average are noisy, and because every measurement weight equally, the noise corrupt the results.

To fix it they make three changes:

- **Jacobian Filtering**: they sort the internal states into height groups per layer, and compute a separate translator for each groups. They are called expert. Some are excellent, some are garbage. So instead of averaging equally, you learn weight that decide which experts gets 0 weight and which is kept.

- **Better gradient calculation**: The take a method called LRP from the earlier R-Lense. With the J-Lens methods, the gradient sizes explode as you move toward early layers. LRP slow that growth a bit. It is mainly accumulated noise.

- Last change is **readout filtering**, which deletes punctuation and whitespace tokens from the results, since they appear on the readouts meaninglessy.

These changes improve the accuracy of the lens. It also find concept earlier in the model, i.e. on previous layer compare to other lens. The swap test also improves. It holds across six models, and the most significant improvements have been found on the two biggest ones.

Of the three change, filtering did most of the job.
